# First 100 customers for a new local service business

*This was the verification run when the council was first built — kept as the working example of the format.*

## Question

What's a low-cost way for a brand-new local service business to get its first 100 customers?

## Chairman's recommendation

Run a three-track sequence — manual outreach seeds the first 10-15 jobs, a referral loop compounds from there, GBP/local listings serve as long-term backstop — but insert an explicit reputation-building step between outreach and referral/GBP, since a brand-new business has zero reviews or social proof to convert on.

**Next actions:**
1. Source the first 10-15 jobs through manual, unpaid outreach (personal network, local Facebook groups, Nextdoor posts, door/flyer drops).
2. After every job, explicitly ask for a Google/Nextdoor review and a photo or short testimonial.
3. Once 5-10 reviews exist, optimize the Google Business Profile listing (photos, reviews, categories).
4. Bake a referral incentive into every completed job from job #1 onward.
5. Log source and cost of each customer; replace guessed conversion rates with real numbers within 30 days.
6. At day 30-60, only test a paid-lead marketplace if the organic pipeline stalls.

**Would change this recommendation:** if the first 10-15 manual-outreach jobs generate zero reviews or referrals — that means the proof-gap is worse than assumed and paid leads need to move up earlier.

## Critic's top risk (carried forward, not resolved away)

Neither the research nor the original strategy addressed the **cold-start, zero-reputation problem** — a brand-new business has no reviews or social proof, which undercuts every GBP/Nextdoor/referral conversion assumption. The plan above exists specifically to close that gap before leaning on proof-dependent channels.
